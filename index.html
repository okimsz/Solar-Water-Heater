SOLARIS / FIELD NOTES

Sunlight → absorber → water → storage.

This is an active, pumped direct-circulation solar water heater. Water from the lower tank flows through the collector's parallel riser tubes and returns near the top. The tank is represented by two mixed layers to show thermal stratification.

The three experiment controls

1. Solar radiation: sets incident power per square metre. At zero radiation, the collector and tank can lose heat to their surroundings.
2. Water flow: changes heat transfer and outlet temperature. Faster flow can transfer more heat while producing a smaller temperature rise on each pass. At zero flow, circulation stops but the absorber can still heat up.
3. Absorber material and surface: metal conductivity changes fin efficiency; coating changes solar absorptance and thermal emittance. Black chrome and matte black have the same assumed absorptance but different emittance, so their heat retention differs.

Model and equations

Collector area A = 2 m²; glass transmittance τ = 0.90; ambient temperature Ta = 25 °C; tank = 80 kg of water in two 40 kg layers; cp = 4180 J/(kg·K). The collector envelope represents about 1.25 × 1.6 m. Other dimensions and pipe routing are illustrative.

Qabsorbed = G A τ α
Qloss = A[3(Tplate − Ta) + εσ(Tplate,K⁴ − Ta,K⁴)]
Fin efficiency ηf = tanh(mL)/(mL), m = √[2h/(kt)], h = 12 W/(m²·K), t = 0.5 mm, L = 45 mm
UA = 180ηf W/K; effectiveness = 1 − exp[−UA/(ṁcp)]
Qwater = ṁcp · effectiveness · (Tplate − Tbottom)
Ccollector dTplate/dt = Qabsorbed − Qloss − Qwater, Ccollector = 18,000 J/K
Tank energy changes by Qwater minus tank heat loss. Each layer has 1.2 W/K loss conductance; layer mixing uses 5 W/K, or 60 W/K during temperature inversion.

Metal conductivity k: copper 385, aluminum 205, steel 50 W/(m·K). Representative coating constants (α, ε): black chrome (0.95, 0.10), matte black (0.95, 0.90), bare metal (0.35, 0.15). These classroom constants are not measured product specifications. No boiling, weather, draw-off, freeze protection or pump electricity is modeled. The simulation stops at the liquid-water model limit; it does not model vapor. Tank storage energy is relative to its initial 25 °C temperature.

Normal time is accelerated by 30×; select 1× for real time. Thermal integration uses small time steps. The pipe markers are intentionally slowed to make flow direction visible. Circulation is manually controlled; an actual installation would normally use a differential-temperature controller.
